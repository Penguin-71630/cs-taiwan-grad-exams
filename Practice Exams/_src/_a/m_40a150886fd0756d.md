(n) Companion-core curve: lowest power at low frequency but rises steeply at high frequency; main-core curves (1 to 4 cores) start higher (leakage) and rise slowly, each extra core shifting the curve up.\
(o) Run on the Companion core at low load; once the needed frequency passes the crossover point, migrate to one main core and raise its frequency with DVFS.\
(p) Use the Companion core for 1 light task; as tasks increase, power on more main cores (up to 4) at low frequency rather than raising frequency; beyond 4 tasks, raise frequency/time-share.\
(q) The lower envelope of all the curves: power grows roughly linearly with performance, since each stage uses the most efficient core configuration.\
(r) Dedicated IPs (GPU, video/audio codecs, ISP, …) do their tasks far more power- and area-efficiently than general-purpose cores, which matters under mobile power, thermal and cost limits.
