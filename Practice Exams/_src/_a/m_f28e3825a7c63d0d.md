(a)(1) Deque $\langle3,1,2,4,5\rangle$: greedy $\langle5,3,4,1,2\rangle$ scores $7$; optimal $\langle3,1,5,2,4\rangle$ scores $9$.\
(a)(2) For deque $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, let $s_{ij}=(-1)^{n-(j-i+1)}$ and $F(i,j)=\max\{s_{ij}x_i+F(i+1,j),s_{ij}x_j+F(i,j-1)\}$, with empty intervals worth $0$. Fill by increasing interval length and backtrack maximizing endpoints: $O(n^2)$ time and space; the example gives $F(1,5)=9$.\
(b)(1) Queue: push onto the input stack; pop from the output stack, transferring all input elements when output is empty. Deque: stack tops represent the two ends; push/pop at the corresponding top, transferring the entire opposite stack when the requested pop stack is empty.\
(b)(2) Charge $3$ per enqueue and $1$ per dequeue; each enqueued element stores two credits for its single transfer. Total cost $\leq3N_{\rm enqueue}+N_{\rm dequeue}=O(n)$.\
(b)(3) False for this whole-stack-transfer deque: $k$ pushes followed by $k$ alternating-end pops can incur $\Theta(k^2)$ stack operations.
