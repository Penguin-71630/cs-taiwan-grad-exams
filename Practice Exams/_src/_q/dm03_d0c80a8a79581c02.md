(A) If $n$ is an integer that is not a multiple of 4, then $n^2 \equiv 1 \bmod 4$.\
(B) A positive integer congruent to 1 modulo 4 cannot have a prime factor congruent to 3 modulo 4.\
(C) There is no such integer $x$ such that $x \equiv 2 \pmod{6}$ and $x \equiv 3 \pmod{9}$\
(D) Assume that $a$, $b$ and $m$ are integers with $m > 1$. If $a \equiv b \pmod{2m}$, then $a \equiv b \pmod{m}$.\
(E) If $n$ is an integer that is not a multiple of 3, then $n^2 \equiv 1 \bmod 3$.
