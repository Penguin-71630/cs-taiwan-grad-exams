1\. Consider a $3\times 3$ matrix $\mathbf{A}$ with *non-zero* elements. Assume $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ has *non-trivial* solutions with two of them being $\mathbf{x} = [1,1,0]^T$ and $[1,0,0]^T$. Which of the following statements are *true* for $\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{y} \in R^3$?

(A) $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$ must have no solution for some $\mathbf{b}$ and exactly one solution for other $\mathbf{b}$.\
(B) $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{y} = [0,0,5]^T$ must have at least one exact solution.\
(C) The least-squares approximation error for the system $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{y} = [0,2,2]^T$ is 4.\
(D) Rank of $\mathbf{A} = 2$.\
(E) $\mathbf{A}$ must have zero rows.
